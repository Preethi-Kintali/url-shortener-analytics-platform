# Module 05 — Stakeholder Communication
## Step 1: CONTEXT

### Part 1: The WHAT — What is a Database Migration?

Imagine you own a bakery and maintain a notebook where you record customer orders, payments, ingredients, and daily sales.

Over time, the notebook becomes difficult to manage, so you decide to move all your records into a better-organized system.

A database migration is similar to moving all your important business records from an old system to a new one without losing any information.

A maintenance window is a planned period during which you temporarily stop accepting or updating certain records so that everything can be moved safely and checked properly.

For example, you might close the bakery's online ordering service for two hours at night while moving the records. After checking that everything is correct, you reopen it.

### Part 2: The WHY — Why Would the Bakery Owner Invest in It?

I would invest in this change because it can help my bakery handle more customers, especially during busy periods such as festivals and holidays.

If my existing system becomes slow when many customers place orders, I might lose customers, delay deliveries, and miss sales opportunities.

Moving to a better system can help me:
- Handle more customer orders during peak hours.
- Reduce delays and improve the customer experience.
- Maintain accurate payment and order records.
- Support future business growth without constantly facing system limitations.

Although temporarily stopping online orders may cause some inconvenience, scheduling the work during a low-demand period can reduce the impact.

In the long run, the investment can help protect revenue, improve customer satisfaction, and prepare the business for future growth.
