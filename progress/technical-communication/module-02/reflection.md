# Module 02 – Final Reflection: Technical Writing Fundamentals

## 1. Structure & Audience Awareness
- **Target Audience (Priya):** Applied a top-down pyramid structure so an onboarding engineer immediately grasps the high-level architectural shift (REST-to-GraphQL migration over 8 weeks) before descending into implementation mechanics, REST limitations, and codebase mapping.
- **Audience Adaptation (Executives vs. Engineers):** Top-down remains the optimal structural paradigm across both audiences, but the content level adjusts: executive communication prioritizes strategic business impact, timelines, resourcing costs, and macro risk mitigations, whereas engineering onboarding focuses on API schema mapping, tooling, and pairing workflows.
- **Structural Commitment:** Reaffirming the top-down model because progressive disclosure accommodates both 30-second skimmers and 10-minute deep readers.

## 2. Durable Technical Writing Principle
- **The 10-Second Headline Test:** Prioritizing the bottom-line decision in the opening sentence ensures that the document communicates its core value immediately, eliminating the risk of a "buried lede."
- **Supporting Disciplines:** Backing up headline clarity with scannable markdown headings, bulleted lists for complex points, and strict active-voice ownership for all action items.

## 3. Core Insights & Surprises
- **Accuracy Does Not Guarantee Usability:** A document can be 100% technically correct while simultaneously failing as an engineering communication artifact due to dense formatting, jargon, and passive phrasing.
- **High-Fidelity Pruning:** Cutting over 33% of words from the original corporate memo removed only throat-clearing fluff and passive padding while preserving 100% of underlying technical facts and constraints.
- **The Hidden Danger of Passive Voice:** Passive constructions disguise accountability, leaving on-call teams and engineers confused about who actually owns a task or mitigation.
